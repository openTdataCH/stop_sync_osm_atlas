# 0.3 Comparing routes

The **Routes** menu has three tabs: the lines of the timetable and OSM, the OSM routes outside the timetable, and the link between timetable stops and ATLAS platforms.

## Lines and their variants

The **Routes** tab shows one card per line ([line family](0.5%20Glossary.md#line-family)), marked **Matched**, **Unmatched ATLAS** or **Unmatched OSM**. The ATLAS side comes from the GTFS timetable, the OSM side from route relations. A card lists the line's variants ([itineraries](0.5%20Glossary.md#itinerary)) by direction, each with a figure:

- *stop overlap* (green): a matched variant. The overlap is the share of stops both variants serve in the same order.
- *Highest overlap* (blue): an unmatched variant's closest variant on the other side; hover to see which. It is a hint, not a match.
- *No shared stops*: no variant on the other side serves any of its stops.

Opening a variant aligns both stop lists in route order and marks a stop that only one side serves, *Not in ATLAS* or *Not in OSM*, or that the sides serve at different platforms of one station. A low overlap or a *Not in OSM* row is a lead: the relation may miss a stop, call at another platform, or follow an older route. Check it before you edit the relation. [E3.2](../engine/documentation/3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#families-that-stay-unmatched) explains why a line stays unmatched.

## Non-GTFS routes

The **Non-GTFS routes** tab lists OSM routes that are not in the Swiss timetable, such as FlixBus lines. They are never matched.

## Timetable stops and ATLAS platforms

The **SLOID ↔ GTFS stop_id** tab shows which ATLAS platform each timetable stop is linked to. That link tells the site which lines serve each platform. Blue marks an ATLAS platform with a timetable stop, orange a timetable stop with a platform, red a platform without a timetable stop and grey a timetable stop without a platform. A wrong link here is not fixed in OSM.
